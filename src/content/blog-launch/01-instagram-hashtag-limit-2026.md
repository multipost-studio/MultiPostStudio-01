The Instagram hashtag limit is 5 hashtags per post, according to Instagram's Help Center as of 2026. Captions can still run to 2,200 characters, but the hashtag count is capped separately. Instagram's developer documentation continues to list 30 for API posts, which is why older guides and newer ones contradict each other.

## Key takeaways

- The Instagram hashtag limit in the Help Center is 5 hashtags per post, and that is the rule that applies when you post in the app.
- Instagram's developer documentation still lists 30 hashtags for the API, but no official source says the API accepts more than the in-app rule allows in practice, so plan around 5.
- The caption limit is 2,200 characters, and hashtags count toward it like any other text.
- With only five slots, relevance beats volume: choose tags that describe the post and the audience you want to reach.
- Checking the count before you publish is easier than discovering a problem afterwards, especially when you schedule posts weeks ahead.

## What is the current Instagram hashtag limit?

Instagram's Help Center says you can use up to 5 hashtags on a post. That is the number to work with. If you paste in 12 tags from an old template, you are outside the published rule, whatever the older advice said.

The confusion comes from two official sources that disagree. The Help Center describes what people can do in the app, and it states 5. The Instagram Graph API reference, which developers use to build publishing tools, still lists 30 hashtags. Nothing official says the API will reject a sixth tag, but nothing says it is safe either. We treat the Help Center figure as the working rule because it is the one written for people who post.

Two other numbers are worth keeping beside it:

- **Caption length:** 2,200 characters. Hashtags, emoji, line breaks and mentions all count toward that total.
- **@-mentions:** up to 20 in a caption when posting through the API.

If your caption is long, a block of tags eats into the space you wanted for the actual message. Five short tags cost very little. Fifteen long ones can cost a hundred characters or more.

## Why do some guides still say 30 hashtags?

Many guides say 30 because Instagram's older documentation and API reference did, and the figure was repeated for years. Advice from that period tends to survive in blog posts, templates and saved hashtag lists long after the rule it described has changed.

Treat any article that states the Instagram hashtag limit as a single confident number, with no date or source, with caution. A few quick checks help:

1. Look for a link to Instagram's own Help Center or developer documentation.
2. Check the publication date, since platform rules change.
3. Test the claim against what the Instagram app lets you do today.

This article is based on Instagram's Help Center and developer documentation as they stood in October 2026. Platform rules change, so check the Help Center if a post behaves unexpectedly.

## How do you choose five hashtags that earn their place?

With five slots, each hashtag should do a specific job. A simple mix works for most accounts, and you can adjust it by niche.

### A practical five-tag mix

- **One broad topic tag** that names the category, such as #skincare or #bookkeeping.
- **Two specific tags** that describe the exact subject of the post, for example #retinolroutine rather than only #beauty.
- **One community or audience tag** that people in your niche actually follow or search.
- **One branded or campaign tag** that you own and use consistently, so your posts can be found together.

This is a starting framework, not a formula Instagram publishes. Your mix may differ. A local business might swap the broad tag for a place name. A creator might drop the branded tag when a post is not part of a series.

### How to check whether a tag fits

Search the hashtag in Instagram before you use it. Look at the top and recent posts. If they do not resemble your content, the tag will not put you in front of the right people, however popular it looks. A tag that is slightly smaller but accurate usually serves you better than a huge one that mixes in unrelated posts.

You can also use a hashtag generator or an AI suggestion for ideas, then cut the list down by hand. Suggestions are candidates, and the final five should come from your judgement about the post.

## Where should hashtags go: caption or first comment?

Instagram's Help Center rule applies to hashtags as a count, and the choice between caption and first comment is a style decision. Putting tags at the end of the caption keeps everything in one place. Putting them in the first comment keeps the caption clean for readers.

If you post by hand, the first comment means opening the post and typing again. If you use a scheduling tool with first-comment auto-posting, the tool can publish the comment for you right after the post goes live. Either way, keep the tag count to five and keep the tags relevant.

One practical point: with only five tags, many people find the caption approach is fine. The case for hiding a long block of tags in a comment gets weaker once the block is short.

## How do you keep scheduled posts within the limit?

The risk with scheduled posts is that you write them days or weeks ahead, paste in a saved tag group, and forget to recount against the Instagram hashtag limit. A saved group built when the old advice applied may hold 20 or 30 tags.

A short routine prevents that:

1. Audit your saved hashtag groups once. Trim every Instagram group to five or fewer.
2. Keep separate groups for each content theme, so you pick one instead of mixing several.
3. Check the count in the composer before you schedule, not after the post is due.
4. Recheck the Help Center page now and then, because platform rules can change.

Writing a different set of tags for each platform also helps. A tag list that suits Instagram is not necessarily right for LinkedIn or YouTube, where the rules differ.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many hashtags can you use on Instagram?

Instagram's Help Center states up to 5 hashtags per post as of 2026. Instagram's developer documentation still lists 30 for the API, but 5 is the safe number to plan around.

### Do hashtags count toward the Instagram caption limit?

Yes. The caption limit is 2,200 characters, and hashtags are part of the caption text, so every tag uses up characters you could have spent on the message.

### Does the 5-hashtag limit apply to Reels and Stories?

Instagram's Help Center describes the limit in terms of posts. We did not find a separate published number for Reels or Stories, so the practical advice is to keep to five tags on any Instagram content.

### Is it better to put hashtags in the caption or the first comment?

Neither is an official requirement, so it comes down to taste. The caption keeps everything together, while a first comment keeps the caption tidy. Choose one and stay consistent.
