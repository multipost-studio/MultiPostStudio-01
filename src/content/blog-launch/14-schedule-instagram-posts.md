To schedule Instagram posts from your computer, switch your account to a Business or Creator account, link it to a Facebook Page, connect it to a scheduling tool, then upload your media, write the caption and pick a publish time. Scheduled posts publish automatically through Instagram's API.

## Key takeaways

- Scheduling through third-party tools needs an Instagram Business or Creator account linked to a Facebook Page, which is a Meta requirement.
- Instagram publishing always needs media. Feed posts, carousels, Reels and Stories each have their own aspect-ratio, count and length rules.
- Captions can run to 2,200 characters, but Instagram's in-app Help Center rule allows up to 5 hashtags per post, so check before you paste a long hashtag block.
- Review the preview and the validation warnings before you schedule, because a fix at drafting time is far easier than a failed post later.

## What you need before you can schedule

Instagram only allows API-based scheduling for professional accounts. In practice that means a Business or Creator account connected to a Facebook Page. This is a requirement set by Meta, not by individual scheduling tools, and a personal account cannot be scheduled through a third-party app.

Switching is done inside Instagram's settings under account type, and it does not delete your posts or followers. Link the account to a Facebook Page you manage, then connect Instagram to your scheduler through Meta's login flow. If the connection step fails, the most common cause is that the Page link is missing or you do not have admin access to the Page.

Also prepare the content itself. Instagram requires an image or video on every post, so a caption on its own cannot be scheduled. Gather your files, the caption, any alt text and the first comment you want pinned beneath the post.

## Choose the right format and check its rules

Each Instagram format has different media requirements. As checked in the MultiPost Studio composer, which follows Instagram's publishing rules at the time of writing:

- **Feed post:** one image or video, with aspect ratios of 1:1, 4:5 or 1.91:1.
- **Carousel:** two to ten images or videos, in 1:1 or 4:5.
- **Reel:** one vertical 9:16 video, between 3 and 90 seconds in the composer's current validation.
- **Story:** one 9:16 image or video. Through the API, stickers, polls and links are not available.

Platform rules change, so treat any numbers as a snapshot and confirm against Instagram's current documentation if a format is critical to your work. The sensible habit is to let your tool check each file before scheduling, so a wrong ratio is flagged while you can still crop it.

Caption length is generous. Instagram allows up to 2,200 characters, but only the opening lines show before the "more" cut-off, so put the point first and the detail after.

## How to schedule Instagram posts step by step

1. **Connect your account.** Link your Business or Creator account in your scheduler's channel settings.
2. **Create a new post.** Choose the format (feed post, carousel, Reel or Story) so the right media rules apply.
3. **Upload media in order.** For a carousel, the first slide is the cover, so order matters.
4. **Write the caption.** Put the hook in the first line. Keep an eye on the live character count.
5. **Add hashtags with care.** Instagram's Help Center states a limit of 5 hashtags per post. Instagram's developer documentation still lists a higher figure for API publishing, so the in-app rule is the safer guide.
6. **Add alt text.** A short, concrete description of the image helps people who use screen readers.
7. **Add a first comment if needed.** This keeps the caption clean and lets you put extra context or a link prompt beneath the post.
8. **Check the preview and warnings.** Fix anything the validation flags.
9. **Pick a date and time,** or add the post to a queue so it takes the next open slot.
10. **Confirm the status.** The post should show as scheduled on your calendar.

## Plan Instagram posts as a system, not one at a time

Scheduling gets more valuable when you batch it. Set aside one session a week to upload and schedule the following week's posts. Place Reels, carousels and single images deliberately so the grid does not end up as seven of the same format.

Choose publish times using your own analytics rather than generic charts. If you do not yet have enough history, start with times you can maintain and compare the results after a few weeks. A simple approach: pick two or three candidate slots, publish similar content in each, then keep the winner.

A content calendar view helps you spot gaps and clusters at a glance. Add a column or label for the content pillar (such as tutorials, customer stories or behind-the-scenes), so the week has variety.

## What can go wrong with scheduled Instagram posts

Most failures are predictable. The connection expires after a password change or a Meta permission change, so reconnect and check the account is still linked to the Page. Media that does not match the format rules gets rejected, which is why pre-checks are useful. And some interactive extras, such as Story stickers, links and polls, cannot be added through the API, so those Stories are best posted natively.

Make a habit of checking your scheduled and failed posts every morning. A failed post is easy to fix if you notice it quickly, and it is only a problem if nobody looks.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I schedule Instagram posts from my computer?

Yes. Connect an Instagram Business or Creator account, linked to a Facebook Page, to a scheduling tool in your browser, then upload media, write a caption and choose a publish time.

### Do I need a Business account to schedule Instagram posts?

You need a professional account, which means Business or Creator, linked to a Facebook Page. Personal accounts cannot be scheduled through third-party tools because of Meta's API rules.

### Can I schedule Reels and carousels, not just photos?

Yes, as long as the files meet the format rules. Reels need a vertical 9:16 video, and carousels need two to ten images or videos in supported ratios.

### How many hashtags can I use on a scheduled Instagram post?

Instagram's Help Center states a maximum of 5 hashtags per post as of 2026. The developer documentation still mentions a higher number for API publishing, so follow the in-app rule.
