To schedule LinkedIn posts, write the post in LinkedIn's own scheduler or a third-party scheduling tool, choose a date and time, and confirm. Pick the time from your own analytics rather than a generic chart: look at which weekdays and hours earned the most engagement from your audience.

## Key takeaways

- You can schedule LinkedIn posts with LinkedIn's built-in options or a third-party scheduler. Check which post types the tool you choose can publish.
- A LinkedIn post can be up to 3,000 characters, but the opening lines decide whether people click to read the rest, so lead with the point.
- Choose publish times from your own engagement history and your audience's working hours and time zone, then test and adjust.
- Schedule the post, not the conversation. Plan time to reply to comments soon after a post goes live.

## Ways to schedule LinkedIn posts

You have two routes. At the time of writing, LinkedIn itself lets you set a future date and time when composing a post, which is convenient for one-off posts from your own profile. Third-party schedulers add a content calendar, queues, previews and team workflows, which matter when you manage several profiles or LinkedIn Pages or publish alongside other platforms.

Whichever you choose, check what the tool can publish. Platform support differs, and gaps often come from LinkedIn's API rather than the tool. For example, MultiPost Studio publishes LinkedIn text posts today, but image and video attachments and LinkedIn articles are not published through it yet. If your plan depends on carousels or documents, confirm support before you commit to a workflow.

Check which account types a scheduler can connect, too. A personal profile and an organisation Page are different publishing targets with different audiences, and not every tool supports both in the same way.

## How to schedule a LinkedIn post, step by step

1. **Connect your account** in your scheduler, authorising it through LinkedIn's login.
2. **Draft the post.** Write directly in the composer and watch the live character count against LinkedIn's 3,000-character limit.
3. **Write the first lines carefully.** LinkedIn collapses long posts behind a "see more" link, so the first couple of lines must give a reason to continue.
4. **Format for scanning.** Use short paragraphs and line breaks. Avoid walls of text.
5. **Add hashtags sparingly.** LinkedIn has no official hashtag limit, and three to five relevant tags is common advice rather than a rule.
6. **Add a first comment if useful.** A first comment can hold a link or a discussion question, keeping the post body focused.
7. **Choose the date and time,** or add the post to your queue.
8. **Preview and confirm,** then check that it appears as scheduled in your calendar.

## What to write: length, structure and hooks

There is no required length. Posts that make one clear point in a few short paragraphs often read best in the feed, though a longer story or a worked example can earn its space. Let the idea set the length, and cut anything that does not serve it.

Start with a specific claim, a question, or a short story rather than a greeting. State the takeaway early, then support it with an example, a lesson or a step-by-step list. End with a clear invitation, such as a question that people with relevant experience can answer.

Be careful with links. Many people place the link in a first comment so the body can stand on its own, but test what works for your audience, because behaviour varies by account and changes over time.

If you post for a company, mix formats: a customer insight, a lesson from the team, a plain explanation of how something works, an occasional product update. A feed made only of announcements is rarely compelling.

## When to schedule LinkedIn posts

LinkedIn is a professional network, so working patterns matter: when your readers take a break, start their day or catch up on messages. That depends on their job, industry and country. A founder talking to engineers in Europe and a recruiter talking to candidates in India will not share an ideal window.

Rather than copy a generic table, find your own posting pattern:

1. Export or review your last twenty to thirty posts with their publish times and engagement.
2. Group them by weekday and hour in one time zone.
3. Compare average engagement rate by slot, ignoring slots with only one post.
4. Choose two or three promising slots and publish similar posts in each over several weeks.
5. Keep the slot that wins most often, and revisit it every quarter.

If you are starting from zero, begin with a steady rhythm you can sustain, such as the same two or three weekdays each week at similar times. Consistency makes it easier to see which results come from timing and which from content. Keep a simple log of each post's weekday, hour and results so the pattern becomes visible.

## Manage LinkedIn Page and personal profile posting

If you publish for both a person and a company, plan them as different voices. Personal posts usually carry opinion and experience, and company Page posts carry updates and resources. Avoid posting identical text on both at the same moment, since followers who see both will notice.

Set a realistic cadence for each. Two or three well-considered posts a week from a profile you can keep up is better than daily posts that drop off after a month. Keep a shared calendar so teammates who post from their own accounts do not collide with your Page schedule.

Finally, build in time after publishing. Replying to early comments, thanking people who add something useful and answering questions is part of the work, and scheduling frees the time to do it properly.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you schedule LinkedIn posts?

Yes. LinkedIn lets you pick a future date and time while composing a post, and third-party scheduling tools can publish LinkedIn posts for you from a content calendar or queue.

### What is the character limit for a LinkedIn post?

A LinkedIn post can be up to 3,000 characters. Only the opening lines show before the "see more" link, so put your main point first.

### How many hashtags should I use on LinkedIn?

LinkedIn has no official limit. Three to five relevant hashtags is common advice, not a rule, so use as many as are genuinely useful and test the effect.

### When is the best time to schedule LinkedIn posts?

It depends on your audience. Compare engagement across weekdays and hours in your own post history, test the top slots for several weeks and keep the best performer.
