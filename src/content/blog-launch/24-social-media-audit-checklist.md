A social media audit is a structured review of every account you run, checking that profiles are accurate, content serves your goals, engagement is healthy and nothing is neglected. You can finish one in a single afternoon by working through the same short checklist for each channel and ending with a ranked list of fixes.

## Key takeaways

- A social media audit has four parts: inventory, profile check, content review and workflow review.
- Use the same checklist for every channel so results are comparable and nothing gets skipped.
- Review the last 20 to 30 posts per channel, not just the best ones, to see your real pattern.
- End the audit with no more than five prioritised actions. A long list of fixes rarely gets done.

## Before your social media audit: set the frame

Block three to four hours and open a blank spreadsheet with one row per channel. Decide the question you want answered, because an audit without a question turns into a tour. Good questions include "Which channels deserve our time next quarter?" and "What is slowing us down?"

Gather your access first: logins or admin roles for each account, your analytics, and your strategy document if you have one. If you can't find who owns an account, that is already your first finding.

## Step 1: Take inventory

List every account your brand holds, including ones you've forgotten. Search your brand name on Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, X, TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest, Threads and Bluesky, and check for any Google Business Profile listing. For each, note:

- Who owns the login and who has admin access
- Whether it is active, dormant or abandoned
- Whether it is official, or a fan, duplicate or impersonation account

Dormant accounts are a quiet risk. They show outdated information under your name. Either revive them, redirect followers to your active channel, or close them.

## Step 2: Check each profile

Open every profile as a stranger would and tick through this list:

1. **Name and handle:** consistent across platforms where possible, so people can find you.
2. **Bio:** says what you do and who it's for, in plain words.
3. **Profile image and banner:** current, legible at small sizes and consistent with your branding.
4. **Link:** points to the right page, works, and carries a UTM tag if you track clicks.
5. **Contact details and hours:** correct, especially for local businesses.
6. **Pinned or featured content:** still relevant, with no expired offers.
7. **Call to action buttons:** present where the platform offers them and pointing to the right place.

Profiles are the cheapest fixes in the audit. Most of these take minutes, and they affect every visitor who clicks through from a post.

## Step 3: Review the content

Scroll through the last 20 to 30 posts on each channel. Look at what you actually published, not what you remember publishing.

### Questions to answer per channel

- **Mix:** do posts spread across your content pillars, or does one theme dominate?
- **Formats:** which formats appear (images, carousels, video, text), and which seem to get more replies or saves?
- **Cadence:** how many posts per week, and how steady? Gaps usually show as streaks of silence.
- **Tone:** does it sound like one voice, or like several people?
- **Platform fit:** do posts respect each platform's norms and limits? As of 2026, for example, Threads caps posts at 500 characters, and X counts standard posts up to 280. A post that was clearly trimmed or truncated suggests a copy-paste workflow.
- **Calls to action:** do posts ask for something specific, or just end?

Mark your three best and three weakest posts, then write one sentence on why you think each landed that way. You are looking for patterns, not conclusions from a single post.

## Steps 4 and 5: Check results, then the workflow

### Step 4: Engagement and traffic

Pull your native analytics for the same period and compare against your goal, not against competitors. Look for these items:

- Reach and follower trend
- Replies, saves and shares, which tend to say more than likes
- Link clicks and website sessions from each channel
- Unanswered comments and messages, and how long they have waited

If you can't tell whether a channel contributes to your goal, that is a finding too. It often means tracking is missing, so check that your links carry UTM parameters, the short labels that tell your analytics tool where a visit came from.

### Step 5: The workflow behind the posts

Many problems in social media come from process, not creativity. Ask your team:

- Where do ideas get stored, and does anyone use that list?
- How far ahead are posts scheduled? Zero days is a warning sign.
- Who approves content, and how long does approval take?
- What happens when someone is on holiday?
- Are hashtags, links and alt text handled consistently?

Write down the single biggest source of friction. Often it is something simple, such as no shared calendar or approvals happening in scattered chat messages.

## Turn findings into an action list

Score each finding on two things: effort (low, medium, high) and impact (low, medium, high). Then choose at most five actions, favouring high impact and low effort. A typical result might look like this:

1. Fix broken or missing links in three profiles (low effort, high impact)
2. Close two abandoned accounts (low effort, medium impact)
3. Set a minimum weekly cadence on the strongest channel (medium effort, high impact)
4. Add UTM tags to every bio link and campaign link (low effort, medium impact)
5. Retire the lowest-value channel for a quarter (medium effort, medium impact)

Give each action an owner and a date. Save the audit as a dated document so you can compare it with the next one in six months.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I run a social media audit?

Run a full audit once or twice a year and a lighter profile check each quarter. Also audit after a rebrand, a team change or a major shift in goals.

### What should a social media audit include?

It should include an account inventory, a profile check, a review of recent content, engagement and traffic results, and a look at your workflow. Each part should end with a clear finding or action.

### How long does a social media audit take?

For three to six channels, a focused audit usually fits in a half day. It takes longer if you have to track down logins or pull analytics from several tools.

### Do I need paid tools for a social media audit?

No. A spreadsheet, each platform's native analytics and your web analytics are enough for a useful audit. Dedicated tools save time when you manage many accounts, but they aren't required.
