Google Business Profile posts are short updates, events and offers that appear on your business listing in Google Search and Maps. Post when you have something useful to say, such as a new offer, an event, a seasonal change or a fresh photo, and keep a steady rhythm you can sustain, such as weekly or fortnightly.

## Key takeaways

- Google Business Profile posts come in three practical types in most scheduling tools, usually: updates, events, and offers. Pick the type that matches your message.
- Posts are written for people who are already looking for a local business, so lead with the practical detail: what, when, where, and what to do next.
- Choose a posting rhythm you can keep for months, because a regular modest cadence beats a burst followed by silence.
- Use one clear photo and one call-to-action button per post, and always check your hours, prices, and dates before publishing.
- Do not expect posts alone to change local rankings. Treat them as helpful information for customers, and check Google's current guidance for details.

## What Google Business Profile posts are for

Google Business Profile (GBP) is the free listing that shows your business details when people search for you on Google or find you in Maps. Posts are a small publishing feature on that listing, letting you add timely information next to your hours, reviews, and photos.

The audience is different from Instagram or Facebook. Someone seeing your GBP post is usually searching for a local service or checking a business they are considering. They are closer to a decision, and less interested in entertainment. That means the best posts are specific and practical rather than clever.

Think of each post as a small shop-window sign. It should tell a passer-by something relevant today: a new menu, an opening-hours change, a booking slot, a discount, or a reason to visit this week.

## The three post types and when to use each

Scheduling platforms that connect to Google Business Profile typically work with three post types: updates, events, and offers. Check which ones your tool supports.

### Updates

An update is a general post with text, an optional image, and an optional call-to-action button. Use it for news that does not have a date range or a discount: new products, staff introductions, behind-the-scenes photos, seasonal reminders, awards, or changes to services.

### Events

An event post has a title and a start and end date and time. Use it for anything with a fixed time: a workshop, a sale weekend, a live music night, a class launch, or a community open day. Because dates are built in, the post makes the timing clear without relying on the caption.

### Offers

An offer post has a title and, optionally, a coupon code, plus dates. Use it for discounts and promotions with clear terms. Be specific about what is included and when it ends, and make sure staff know about the offer before it goes live.

## What to post: ideas by business type

If you run out of ideas, borrow from this list and adapt it to your own business. These are examples, not guaranteed formulas.

- **Restaurants and cafes:** a new seasonal dish, a weekday lunch special, holiday opening hours, an event night.
- **Salons and clinics:** a newly available appointment type, a seasonal service, an introduction to a team member, a reminder about booking.
- **Retail shops:** new arrivals, a restock of a popular item, a local-event tie-in, an in-store workshop.
- **Trades and home services:** a seasonal checklist, a recent job (with the customer's permission), the areas you now cover, availability for urgent calls.
- **Fitness studios:** a new class timetable, a beginner course, a trial offer, a community challenge.

Across all of them, the strongest posts answer a question a customer might type into Google: Are you open on the holiday? Do you do this service? Is there a deal this week?

## How to write a post people act on

A good GBP post is short, concrete, and finished before the reader loses interest. Follow this structure.

1. **Lead with the point.** Put the main detail in the first line, since previews may cut off longer text.
2. **Add specifics.** Include dates, times, prices, and the location, if relevant.
3. **Use one clear image.** Posts typically carry a single image, so pick a sharp, real photo of your products, premises, or team over a generic graphic. Add a useful description if the platform asks.
4. **Choose one call-to-action.** Pick the single next step you want, such as booking, calling, ordering, or learning more, and link to a page that fits it.
5. **Check the details.** Confirm hours, spelling, links, and pricing before publishing.

Avoid stuffing keywords or listing every service you offer. Write for the customer first. Natural wording that includes your service and location is enough.

## How often to post

Google does not publish a required posting frequency that we can point to, so choose a cadence based on what you realistically have to say. For many small businesses, one post a week or one every two weeks is manageable, but treat that as a starting suggestion, not a rule.

A simple rhythm, as an illustrative example:

1. **Weekly:** one update with a photo, such as a new item, a team moment, or a seasonal reminder.
2. **Monthly:** one event or offer tied to something happening that month.
3. **Whenever something changes:** a short update for holiday hours, closures, or new services.

If you manage several locations or clients, use a content calendar and schedule posts in a batch, then review them before they go live. Local details differ by location, so avoid duplicating the same post across branches without adjusting hours, addresses, and offers.

### Keep posts fresh and accurate

Old posts with expired offers or past events can confuse customers. Review your live posts regularly and remove or replace anything out of date. Always keep your core profile information, such as hours, phone number, and address, accurate as well, since posts sit beside it.

## Measure and learn

Google provides performance information for business profiles, and your own data shows what happens afterwards. Track actions that matter to the business: calls, direction requests, website visits, bookings, and redemptions of a coupon code.

Using a distinct coupon code on offer posts is a simple way to see whether the post prompted visits. For links, add UTM parameters (tracking tags that identify the source in your web analytics) so you can see traffic from a specific post in your analytics reports.

Review results every month or so. Look for patterns, such as offers outperforming general updates, and let those inform the next month's plan. Keep the sample size in mind: a handful of posts will not prove much, so avoid sweeping conclusions.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are Google Business Profile posts?

They are short updates, events, and offers that you publish on your Google Business Profile listing. They can appear to people who find your business on Google Search or Maps.

### How often should you post on Google Business Profile?

There is no official required frequency that we can cite. Many small businesses aim for a weekly or fortnightly post, plus extra updates when hours or offers change. Choose a rhythm you can maintain.

### Can you schedule Google Business Profile posts?

Yes, many scheduling tools can publish to Google Business Profile, so you can plan updates, events, and offers ahead of time. Review each post before it goes live, especially dates and prices.

### Do Google Business Profile posts help local SEO?

Posts give customers useful information, but we cannot promise they improve local rankings. Focus on accurate profile details, reviews, and helpful content, and check Google's own guidance for current advice.
